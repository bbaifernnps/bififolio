Shopping discovery.

01

Overview

Live and social-commerce sellers were building real businesses on Facebook and Vrich, with zero presence inside TrueMoney — even though many of their buyers already pay through the app. Shopping Discovery is a new page in TMN that surfaces these sellers by category, so buyers can find them without ever leaving their wallet.

02

The problem

Live sellers had no visibility inside TrueMoney, and TMN buyers already active in live/social shopping had no bridge from the app they pay with to the sellers they'd want to pay. The brief I inherited was three business slides — a CMS wireframe, a frontend flow, and an overview — with no formal Experience Canvas and almost no user-side rationale, only the business case.

Business-first briefStrong business case, thin user-need rationale — mine to draft and validate

03

Goals & constraints

Grow seller visibility and turn it into TMN P2P volume: the business case targets 2,000 pilot sellers, projected to bring in ~83,678 new P2P senders through network effect. Constraint: this project skips the usual PL review step entirely — designs go straight to leadership and business — so there's less buffer to catch gaps before a senior review.

04

My process

1

Discover

Since no Experience Canvas existed, I drafted one myself. Pulled seller data (Vrich sellers skew Fashion 35% and Beauty & Skincare 25% — the highest-transacting categories) and surfaced a buyer persona business hadn't put in the original deck: ages 40–60, who shop by watching live sessions on Facebook.

2

Define

Confirmed the actual interaction model with business: TMN never plays video — it shows a static thumbnail and taps straight out to the seller's own Facebook or Instagram. TMN's role is discovery and listing only. Eligibility is a firm gate: a seller must both be paid through TrueMoney and be Vrich-partnered.

3

Design

Placed the entry point on TMN's "all services" icon grid per business decision, and ordered category shelves by real transaction data — Fashion and Beauty first, not alphabetically. Designed a How-To section explaining how to link a bank account and transfer to it.

4

Test & refine

Caught a real tension against the 40–60 buyer persona: the original wireframe used small, dense badge tags, which doesn't work for an older audience that needs larger type and bigger tap targets. I'd also initially recommended sending the How-To topics to separate screens — re-checking that against the same persona showed it added unnecessary navigation, so I reworked it into one inline accordion with a numbered step timeline, right on the discovery page.

Process artefactsExperience Canvas, seller/buyer data — coming with the Figma pass

05

The solution

A discovery feed, not a marketplace. Category shelves lead with Fashion and Beauty & Skincare, matching where the transaction volume actually is. Tapping a seller's static thumbnail exits straight to their own Facebook or Instagram — TMN stays the discovery layer, not a media player. An inline How-To accordion at the bottom walks people through linking and transferring to a bank account, in place instead of navigating away.

08

What I learned

Given a business-goal-only brief, drafting the missing Experience Canvas myself was the only way to get user-side rationale into the room at all. And I learned to keep checking my own recommendations against the actual persona — the navigate-to-separate-screens idea sounded reasonable until I re-tested it against a 40–60-year-old buyer who just wants the answer inline, right where they already are.
